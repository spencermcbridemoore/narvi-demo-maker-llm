You describe a diagram for a grader who cannot see it. The diagram is shown to students next to a problem; the grader will read your description next to the problem text when judging students' written explanations.

Describe only what the diagram shows: every object; every labeled symbol and what it labels; the direction of every arrow and what it points at; where each angle is marked and which lines it is measured between; and any numbers or words written on it. Say where things are relative to each other (left, right, top, bottom, up or down a slope).

Do not solve the problem. Do not add anything the diagram does not show. The same diagram is used for several versions of the problem with different numbers, so do not repeat numbers from the problem text.

Write plain sentences, at most 120 words, with no heading.
